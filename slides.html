Money is too effective in campaigns

Financial capital in political campaigns directly translates into narrative dominance because low voter civics literacy, media fragmentation, and partisan affinity make emotional, high-frequency advertising far more effective than non-partisan information or fact-checking.

The Paradox

Why this stays stuck

A healthy democracy demands an open, financially unrestricted marketplace of speech; yet those exact market mechanics allow concentrated financial capital to buy narrative control, overwhelming the truth and eroding the voter autonomy necessary for self-governance.

Who benefits from the status quo: Ad-Tech & Broadcast Media Outlets: Receive billions in guaranteed, high-margin revenue every election cycle regardless of who wins. Incumbent Politicians & Specialized Interest Groups: Benefit from high financial entry barriers that insulate them from grassroots challengers. Political Campaign Agencies: Receive fixed percentages (often 10–15%) of total media buy spend, creating a direct incentive to urge clients to raise and dump more cash into ads.
